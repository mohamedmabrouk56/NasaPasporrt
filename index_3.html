Why you're here

Checkpoint 01

The Mission

NASA Space Apps Cairo connects people here with the innovation work around the global Space Apps Challenge, and gives them room to build. The bootcamp is the run-up to that.

One day to understand what the challenges actually ask for, to pick up the craft you'll need, and to walk into the hackathon weekend with far fewer unknowns than you arrived with.

  • 01Understand the challenge
    What's being asked, what data sits behind it, what a real answer looks like.
  • 02Pick up the craft
    Eighteen hands-on sessions across development, data, design, GIS, AI and storytelling.
  • 03Learn to land it
    Presenting, critical thinking, and what separated winning projects from the rest.

The solution stays yours. Nobody here builds it for you — that's the whole point.

Support & its limits

Checkpoint 03

The Crew

Volunteers run the floor all day, so that nothing between you and the sessions costs you the thing you came for.

  • ASKDirections and logistics
    Halls, session rooms, restrooms, prayer areas, water points, the nearest shop, photography spots.
  • ASKGetting connected
    There's a hand available if you can't get onto the venue network. Ask rather than burning twenty minutes.
  • LIMITUnderstanding a challenge
    They can unpick a brief you're reading wrong. They won't decide your approach or design your solution.
  • RULERoom and access calls
    When a volunteer closes a hall or caps a room, it's a capacity decision for the whole day. Please follow it.
Mentor link

Fig. 5 — Mentor contact

Hackathon weekend

Checkpoint 03b

Mentor Link

The bootcamp is speakers and workshops. Mentors — the people who sit with your team while you build — come in on the hackathon weekend.

  • 1Prepare the question
    Mentor time is short and shared. Arrive with a specific blocker, not "can you look at our project?"
  • 2Meet the right specialism
    Mentors are recruited against the challenge tracks, so the person you get should know your field.
  • 3Take the guidance
    They review direction, sanity-check an approach, unstick a technical problem.
  • 4Apply it yourselves
    Mentors don't build the solution. The work and the credit stay with your team.
